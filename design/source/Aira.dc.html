AIRA: one gentle companion for living with PCOS

Women with PCOS juggle a cycle tracker, a sleep tracker, a calorie counter, the internet and their doctor, and most of them quit within weeks. We researched why, and designed AIRA: a single ecosystem that connects sleep, nutrition, movement, stress and cycle, and supports rather than judges.

01 · The problem

One in 10 women experience PCOS. Few get day-to-day support.

Managing PCOS means paying attention to sleep, food, movement, stress and the cycle at once. Today each of those lives in a different place, and none of them talk to each other.

Cycle tracker Sleep tracker Calorie counter The internet Medical counsel

The result is a fragmented experience: more logging, less understanding, and apps abandoned after a couple of weeks.

02 · Research goals

Three questions we set out to answer

  1. 1How do women manage PCOS across fragmented tools, and what causes tracker fatigue and app abandonment?
  2. 2How can we connect isolated data like sleep, nutrition and cycle to uncover the reasons behind daily symptoms?
  3. 3What features and tone turn a health tracker from judgmental into an empathetic, supportive companion?
03 · Research methods

Secondary research to frame it, people to ground it

Secondary

04 · What we heard

In their words

"I'd use it diligently for about two weeks, then stop... I don't have the brain space to think of alternatives."
"I'd love an energy management system... I'd want to track my energy and log when it went down, what made it go up."
"There have been times where I think, 'Why am I so anxious?' and I open the app and see it's mid-cycle."
"Before, I'd feel guilty about eating more... Now I think, 'Maybe I need to up my food intake,' not starve."
"A lot of people don't understand. Even my parents are like, 'Get on with life.'"
"If there was a quick audio or a simple color to choose... that would help."
05 · Themes

Four patterns across interviews

Cycle awareness as power

Knowing where they are in their cycle turns self-blame into explanation.

The tracker fatigue cycle

Strong start, no visible shift, quiet abandonment after about two weeks.

The gap: energy management

They want to understand their energy, not log another meal.

Symptoms are emotional, not just physical

Overwhelm, anxiety and feeling misunderstood are part of the condition.

06 · Survey insights

Why habits fail

  • The habit feels too big or strict
  • I forget
  • I don't see immediate results
  • I lose motivation
  • I start strong but can't stay consistent
  • Life and work get too busy

08 · Personas

Who we designed for

Melanie

The overwhelmed go-getter

"I feel like I'm fighting my own body every single day."

She starts every Monday strong. By Wednesday a deadline hits, cravings spike and she falls off the wagon, then blames her willpower when she is really fighting her biology.

Frustrations
  • Logging every meal feels like a second job
  • Missing a workout feels like failing
  • Her period app doesn't know she's stressed
Needs
  • Energy management, not micro-management
  • Compassionate consistency that adapts to bad days
  • The link between sleep, cravings and cycle

Aisha

The tired optimist, 26

"It's hard to plan my life when my body has its own schedule."

Balancing work and grad school, her energy swings unpredictably. She blames herself for inconsistency, but she is fighting hormone-driven fatigue, cravings and stress.

Frustrations
  • Invisible symptoms: "I look fine, but I'm wiped out"
  • All-or-nothing habits: one off day and she quits
  • No forecast for fatigue or cravings
Needs
  • Energy forecasting for each phase
  • Gentle tracking: zero guilt, zero pressure
  • One helpful suggestion, not a checklist
09 · Behavior design

Designing with the Fogg Behavior Model

A behavior happens when motivation, ability and a prompt meet at the same moment. Our users had motivation; what broke was ability on low-energy days, and prompts that felt like nagging.

B=M·A·P
Motivation
Celebrate first, so small wins feel worth repeating.
Ability
One glanceable score and one-tap logging instead of a checklist.
Prompt
Tapered nudges that read as invitations, not commands.

11 · The concept

One ecosystem, built around hormonal harmony

AIRA connects sleep, nutrition, movement, stress and cycle into a single picture, so a hard day comes with a reason and one next step.

Hormonal Harmony Score

One number for today, with a plain-language reason behind it.

Phase-based recommendations

Suggestions that change with each menstrual phase.

Sleep, energy and mood tracking

Quick logging that answers back with encouragement and context.

12 · Beyond the app

Meeting users where they are

Widget: passive awareness

  • Keeps Aira present without demanding attention
  • A single glanceable metric removes "where do I even start" paralysis
  • The mascot carries emotional continuity from home screen to app

Notifications: active invitation

  • Celebrate first ("Nice work getting some movement in") before asking for anything
  • Reflection prompts are invitations, not commands
  • Part of a tapered nudge system for weeks 3–6 maintenance
