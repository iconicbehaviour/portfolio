EGIS Innovation Challenge · Strategy project · Jan – May 2025

Green Roof Hubs: cooling Flatbush, one roof at a time

Flatbush and Nostrand Avenue in Brooklyn face intense heatwaves and street flooding. We proposed a network of green roofs on public buildings, monitored by AI sensors and paired with a community app, that cools homes, absorbs rain and turns rooftops into classrooms.

01 · Why it's personal

We live in the neighborhood we designed for

One summer afternoon on Nostrand Avenue, I met an elderly woman struggling to breathe on her walk home. Her building had no AC, and she often sat by her fridge just to stay cool. That moment made the climate crisis feel real, and local.

"We've lived the heat, seen the gaps, and we're building a cooler, greener future, starting on our own rooftops."
02 · The problem

Older buildings, little green space, and rising heat

Aging infrastructure, low-income households and limited public cooling leave Flatbush residents especially exposed to four climate hazards.

Up to 7°F
Urban heat island
hotter in NYC neighborhoods with little vegetation and lots of asphalt
Stormwater flooding
Frequent street flooding during storms because of outdated drainage.
Energy burden
Vulnerable residents in older buildings, many without air conditioning.
Biodiversity loss
Fewer pollinators and native plants as the city builds out.

Source: NYC Mayor's Office of Sustainability

03 · The solution

Green Roof Hubs, with AI-powered education

Mission: enhance local climate resilience and raise awareness. Vision: become a global benchmark for sustainability.

Each rooftop hub

  • Cools buildings and reduces urban heat
  • Absorbs rainwater and prevents street flooding
  • Restores biodiversity with native plants
  • Educates the public through hands-on workshops
  • Tracks impact with AI and satellite data

What makes it smart

AI soil and moisture sensors
Monitor roof health and water retention.
Gamified impact dashboard
Shows each neighborhood's climate impact.
Satellite imaging
Google Earth Engine measures green coverage over time.
04 · How it works

From one rooftop to a neighborhood movement

  1. 1Select rooftops on public schools, libraries and NYCHA buildings
  2. 2Train local youth in green infrastructure with partners like Green City Force
  3. 3Install green roofs and monitor soil and temperature with AI sensors
  4. 4Launch an app to track actions like composting and planting
  5. 5Reward the neighborhoods with the most participation and impact
05 · Community and equity

Built with the community, not for it

  • Partner with Green City Force and GrowNYC to train and hire local youth
  • Prioritize buildings in heat-vulnerable and flood-prone zones
  • Offer stipends and skill certifications for long-term economic opportunity
  • Design workshops with input from multilingual, multigenerational residents

07 · Feasibility

Why this can happen now

  • NYC already funds green infrastructure through its Green Roof Tax Abatement Program
  • Legal frameworks for retrofitting public rooftops are in place
  • Partners across tech (IBM, Google.org), CSR sponsors (Patagonia, Seventh Generation) and city agencies (NYC DEP, NYCHA)
  • Green roofs are proven in Chicago, Toronto and Copenhagen

09 · Proposed rollout

Pilot, partner, launch, scale

This was a strategy project; we did not implement it. The plan below is the roadmap we proposed to the judges.

  1. Summer 2025
    Pilot at one NYCHA building in Flatbush
  2. Ongoing
    Secure partnerships with CSR-aligned brands and local NGOs
  3. Fall 2025
    Launch the community challenge and app
  4. End of 2026
    Scale to 5 rooftops and 500 participants
10 · Reflection

What I took away

This project taught me to apply human-centered design to a civic idea. Green roofs already exist in cities around the world. What made ours innovative was the social layer: a way for neighbors to learn, take part and connect with each other through their rooftops.

It reinforced why I chose a career in tech in the first place: to use technology for good.
