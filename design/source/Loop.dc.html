Loop: a running app about stories, not just stats

Runners lose motivation when their apps only count miles. Loop is a social running app that connects runners before, during and after races, so they can share progress, motivate each other and celebrate milestones together.

My role
Product manager and designer
Team
Team of four
Methods
Survey, interviews, personas, task flows
Tools
Figma, Figma Slides
01 · The problem

Apps track the run. Nobody connects the runner.

Runners struggle to stay motivated when apps focus on stats, not stories. And no single platform connects runners socially across training, recovery and race days.

Where running apps fall short

Belonging Connection Ongoing engagement Competition Coaching Coordination
02 · Survey

What runners told us

70%
use Strava or Nike Run Club, but only for tracking, not connection
57%
say motivation drops when they train solo
50%
value community support as motivation
40%
say social features like clubs and group chats are key for connection
30%
avoided or left online running communities because of comparison or pressure
20%
actively share their runs on existing platforms

The gap: runners want community, but today's apps are built for tracking, and public comparison pushes some away.

The community runner

06 · The concept

Six things Loop lets runners do

Connect with nearby runners and marathon groups
Track runs and progress, and set goals
Find local races, group runs and post-run events
Tell their story with captions, notes, videos and reflections
Share gear tips, training advice and event recaps
Celebrate milestones and keep each other accountable
07 · Task flows

Four core journeys

Onboarding

  1. Enter name, username and bio
  2. Set goals: distance, frequency, pace, event
  3. Choose a level: beginner, intermediate or advanced
  4. Optionally log past achievements

Activity and sharing

  1. Scroll the activity feed and like posts
  2. Tap "+" to share
  3. Upload a video of a sunset run and add a caption
  4. Post and return to the feed

Connecting

  1. Open Discover to find running partners nearby
  2. Send a connect request, or
  3. View a profile and send a message

Finding a marathon group

  1. Search the marathon in Discover
  2. If the group exists, ask to join and post
  3. If not, create it with name, date and location
  4. Invite and connect with other runners

09 · Reflection

What I took away

I owned the product strategy and UX flow for Loop. I drew inspiration from how run clubs operate on social media today and looked closely at where existing apps fail at community. Then I designed matching around the attributes runners truly care about, so connections help them sustain healthy habits.

Designing for both social runners and private ones like Ana taught me that community can't be one-size-fits-all. Some people run for the group, others run to be alone, and a good product lets each feel at home without pressure to perform. Matching on what runners actually care about, and letting people choose how visible they are, kept habits healthy for both.
