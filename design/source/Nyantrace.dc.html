NyanTrace: governance for AI agents that are technically correct, but behaviorally wrong

Teams can orchestrate AI agents and trace them, but nothing governs what those agents are allowed to do once they're live. As co-founder and CPO, I led customer discovery, turned it into the MVP PRD, and shaped NyanTrace into the governance layer for agents in production: audit trails, policy enforcement, human approvals and kill switches.

01 · The problem

Agents are making decisions. Nobody is governing them.

AI isn't just layering onto systems of record anymore; it's replacing the logic inside them. Gartner predicts that by 2028, 15% of day-to-day work decisions will be made autonomously by agentic AI.

Frameworks like LangGraph and CrewAI orchestrate agents, and tools like LangSmith trace them. But no standard layer controls what they do once deployed: which tools they can call, when a human must approve, and how to prove afterwards what happened.

Orchestrate→Trace→Govern: the missing layer

03 · What we found

Teams are flying blind

Critical · 4 of 5 interviews

Sub-agents are an observability black hole

When an agent spawns sub-agents, nobody can see what they do. Tracing that microservices take for granted doesn't exist for agents.

"My agent spawns three sub-agents in parallel and I have no idea what they're doing until I see the final output."
Critical · 3 of 5

Policy is written in prompts, and it fails

Rules live in system prompts that drift and that models can ignore. Advisory, not enforced.

"Our policy is to always escalate to a human if the confidence is below 70%. We enforce this in the system prompt. But there's no guarantee the model follows it."
Highest severity · 2 of 5

Liability without evidence

Companies shipping agents to enterprises are liable for agent behavior but can't prove what their agents did, and that blocks deals.

"Enterprise customers ask: can you prove what your agent did and didn't do? Right now I have no answer."
No cost attribution
No one knew what an agent run cost, per person or per session.
Fragmented tools
Different teams on different AI tools, each with its own dashboard.
Misuse found weeks late
Usually through a bill spike, not a governance mechanism.
Trust needs a visible path
People can't hand agents real autonomy when they can't see the reasoning.
04 · What challenged our assumptions

Where I was wrong going in

Governance is a compliance need
Developers want tracing to build trust in their agents. Compliance comes after.
So we led with "see what your agent does," not "stay compliant."
Gamification and governance are separate
One team's token leaderboard was their only governance tool.
So the dashboard should feel like an insight tool, not surveillance.
Operators value governance for its own sake
For platform operators, governance is how they close enterprise deals.
So it's a revenue feature, and should be priced like one.
Willingness to pay needs pricing questions
A CTO said "I would pay for this" without being asked.
That made his team our first design-partner candidate.
05 · From insight to MVP

Six opportunities, each with a smallest bet

I ranked opportunities by evidence strength, severity and personas affected, then defined the smallest thing we could ship to test each one.

OpportunityMVP bet
1. Sub-agent visibilityA session ID carried through every sub-agent, shown as a parent-child trace
2. Programmatic policyPolicies checked before each tool call, with block and warn modes
3. Cost and usage for managersA weekly cost email per engineer and an alert on sensitive file access
4. Compliance audit exportA one-session incident report, on a tamper-evident hash chain from day one
5. Cross-tool viewConnectors for two AI coding tools on one normalized event schema
6. Developer trustA session trace explorer, plus a diff between runs for debugging regressions

The adoption loop we designed for: see more → trust more → automate more.

06 · The MVP PRD

What I specified

A Python SDK, a real-time behavior engine and a dashboard with emergency controls. Just as important were the non-goals: no policy authoring UI, no detailed cost reporting, no deep SIEM integrations in v1.

One-line instrumentation
A Python decorator captures LLM calls, tool use and agent-to-agent messages.
Session tracing
The full execution path of a single agent or a multi-agent session.
Risk scoring and drift alerts
A live risk score per agent and session, with threshold alerts.
Kill switch
Halt an agent from the dashboard or a Telegram command, with the event logged.
Hard-invariant policies
Block critical violations, like emitting PII, right in the runtime path.
Cost data foundation
Token and API usage captured per agent, ready for attribution later.

08 · Where it landed

"Ship AI agents your compliance team will actually approve."

NyanTrace went into private beta as the governance layer for AI agents: framework-agnostic, model-neutral, built around three use cases.

Financial agents
Per-agent spending caps, human approval before trades, a tamper-proof entry for every transaction.
Customer support QA
Sensitive escalations pause for review; low-risk actions like password resets go through.
Internal data security
Row-level policies stop RAG agents from surfacing PII, on any model.
